01 / Business & client view

Executive overview

A single research-and-trading experience where visitors explore the market and understand AI forecasts; authenticated users can monitor portfolios and run autonomous paper simulations; live trading becomes available only after explicit authorization and regulatory readiness.

Public researchInstrument discovery, charts, filters, AI rationale, news context.
Realistic simulationVirtual cash, market hours, bid/ask spread, costs, orders and P&L.
Measured intelligenceCalibrated forecasts, uncertainty, outcomes, drift and audit trails.
Controlled live executionOpt-in, broker linkage, risk engine, kill switch, reconciliation.
Non-negotiable: The system cannot know future stock prices or guarantee profits. Prediction outputs are conditional probability estimates. No unattended real-money execution in the initial learning release.

Business success measures

  • Market coverage and feed freshness, with gaps made visible rather than silently hidden.
  • Prediction quality versus simple benchmarks, evaluated out of sample and net of costs.
  • Paper-trading fidelity: modeled versus realized execution when later tested with small authorized live orders.
  • Operational safety: no duplicate orders, no unbounded loss, full trade and model traceability.

04 / Market data

Instrument coverage and tick ingestion

“Every stock, every tick” is an ingestion objective, not an instruction to run a large AI model on each packet. Maintain the exchange instrument master and subscription plan; consume live packets for entitled instruments; update rolling features incrementally; run expensive inference only for candidates and material events.

Daily instrument sync
Load Dhan security IDs, exchange segments, symbols, trading status and corporate-action adjustments.
Live subscriptions
Allocate instruments within broker connection limits; monitor disconnects, heartbeat, packet timestamps and resubscription.
Normalize and aggregate
Map provider payloads into a canonical schema; derive 1-second/1-minute candles, turnover, volume and volatility.
Hot versus cold storage
Cache latest prices; store required candles in PostgreSQL; archive compressed tick data to Blob only if license and economics permit.
Quality and recovery
Detect gaps, stale prices, bad ticks, missing sessions and corporate actions; halt decisions for affected instruments until reconciled.
Licensing gate: Dhan API subscription, historical access, exchange redistribution rights, news content rights and public quote display are separate questions. Do not expose proprietary feed data publicly until rights are confirmed.

Market session rules

Use the official NSE/BSE trading calendar, exchange holidays, special sessions and segment-specific hours. For ordinary NSE cash trading, the regular session is typically 09:15–15:30 IST. Paper simulation must honor market close, auction phases, circuit limits, illiquidity and settlement assumptions.

Data correctness rules

  • Store both published_at and first_seen_at; backtests may only use what was actually available at decision time.
  • Use primary sources first: exchange disclosures, central banks and official economic releases; secondary media provides context.
  • Cross-check high-impact breaking events, assign confidence and expiry, and do not trade on unverified rumors.
  • Account for market hours, time zones, overnight news and revisions to macroeconomic data.
  • Track event-to-company relationships as probabilistic, not guaranteed causal impacts.
06 / Quantitative AI

Prediction architecture and evaluation

Primary numeric model

LightGBM for cross-sectional ranking and expected return/direction on fixed horizons such as 5, 15 and 30 minutes.

Independent challenger

XGBoost and simple benchmarks (momentum, mean reversion, no-trade) for comparative validation.

Financial language

FinBERT or domain-tuned classifier for sentiment; a low-cost LLM for event extraction, research summaries and human-readable rationale.

Model governance

MLflow or lightweight model registry, immutable datasets, walk-forward tests, shadow deployment, drift checks and rollback.

Features

Price/volume returns, relative volume, spread, VWAP distance, volatility, trend, sector strength, market breadth, Nifty/Bank Nifty regime, global lagged returns, FX and commodity shocks, company filings, sentiment, liquidity and calendar effects. Respect source publication and ingestion timestamps.

Outputs per symbol and horizon

{
  "symbol": "EXAMPLE",
  "asOf": "2026-10-28T10:15:00+05:30",
  "horizonMinutes": 15,
  "probabilityUp": 0.58,
  "expectedReturnBps": 12,
  "predictionIntervalBps": [-35, 52],
  "marketRegime": "high_volatility",
  "dataFreshnessMs": 500,
  "modelVersion": "lgbm-v0.3",
  "action": "NO_TRADE",
  "reason": "Expected edge does not clear costs and risk threshold"
}

Illustrative schema and invented example values, not a real stock forecast.

Training and deployment

  1. Build historical point-in-time feature snapshots and labels at fixed horizons.
  2. Split chronologically; use purged/embargoed walk-forward validation to reduce leakage from overlapping labels.
  3. Measure calibration, precision on top-ranked opportunities, net returns after costs, drawdown, turnover and stability across regimes.
  4. Run challenger models in shadow mode on live data; compare against champion and baseline.
  5. Require explicit approval for promotion; log model hash, training window, metrics and rollback target.
AI policy: Never let a language model directly submit an order. It can extract evidence and suggest a signal; a deterministic strategy and risk engine must independently authorize every order.
07 / Execution & simulation

Two isolated trading engines

PAPER MODE — DEFAULT

Virtual order execution

  • Virtual accounts, available cash, reserved margin and positions.
  • Market/limit/stop order state machine.
  • Latency, spread, slippage, partial fills, queue uncertainty and no-fill conditions.
  • Indian brokerage, statutory charges and taxes as configurable, versioned rules.
  • Session cutoff, square-off, circuit filters, liquidity and leverage restrictions.
  • Immutable fills, ledger, realized/unrealized P&L, daily summaries.
LIVE MODE — GATED

DhanHQ order execution

  • Explicit user opt-in and broker authorization.
  • Static outbound IP and broker requirements validated.
  • Pre-trade risk: max position, max loss, liquidity, exposure and allowed instruments.
  • Idempotent client order IDs, rate limits and state reconciliation.
  • Kill switch, circuit breakers and manual intervention.
  • Broker truth is authoritative for fills, positions and balances.

Risk rules (initial defaults for research)

ControlExample starting ruleImplementation
Capital₹1,00,000 virtualPer-paper-account ledger
Deployable capital60% of virtual cashReserved balance / buying-power check
Concurrent positions5 maximumAtomic position limit
Daily stop2% of starting capitalKill new orders; policy-driven flattening

These are configurable design defaults, not validated optimal risk parameters. Any leverage/margin must be modeled according to actual broker product eligibility and exchange rules; it cannot be assumed available.

08 / Sequence flows

End-to-end processing

Flow A — market tick to AI insight

Flow B — autonomous paper trade

Strategy receives versioned prediction, signal context and current virtual portfolio state.
Deterministic risk engine evaluates position size, daily loss, liquidity, costs, session, stale data and existing orders.
Paper OMS records a pending order with unique ID; fill simulator uses contemporaneous bid/ask or defensible candle-based assumptions.
Ledger atomically records fills, fees, cash changes, positions and P&L. User dashboard receives updates.
At session end, reconcile positions, produce a report, attach outcomes to predictions and schedule offline model evaluation.

Flow C — gated live trade

Partitioning and retention

  • Partition candles by date and instrument/exchange strategy after benchmarking; index on (instrument_id, event_time DESC).
  • Keep recent hot bars in PostgreSQL; compact older history to columnar Parquet in Blob for offline training.
  • Use immutable event/audit logs, encrypted broker tokens, backups, tested restoration and retention aligned to applicable obligations.
  • Separate paper and live order tables, account identifiers, credentials and service permissions to prevent mode confusion.

12 / Security & regulation

Defense in depth and legal launch gates

Identity and application

OAuth/OIDC, email verification, MFA for sensitive actions, least-privilege RBAC, secure cookies, CSRF protection, input validation, WAF/rate limiting and dependency scanning.

Broker and secrets

Keep access tokens server-side, encrypt with managed keys/Key Vault, restrict egress IP, rotate secrets, isolate accounts and prohibit browser-side trading credentials.

Trading safety

Independent pre-trade risk checks, idempotency, broker reconciliation, daily loss controls, stale-feed halts, strategy allowlists, audit logs and emergency stop.

Operational security

Managed identities, private networking when justified, encrypted backups, CI/CD permissions, vulnerability alerts, incident runbooks and recovery drills.

Regulatory dependency: Obtain current SEBI/NSE/BSE and Dhan confirmation for retail algo trading, third-party platform/API arrangements, exchange data display/redistribution, strategy registration or approvals where required, static-IP restrictions, audit/record retention, and investment-advice/research regulations. This blueprint is not a legal determination.

Provide disclosures for model uncertainty, historical performance limitations, simulated versus live results, data freshness and the nature of any recommendations. If distributing personalized trading advice, get specialist regulatory advice before launch.

13 / Cloud deployment

Azure-first, cost-conscious infrastructure

Environment plan

  • Local: Docker Compose for API, Python, Postgres and optional Redis; replay historical market sessions.
  • Development: One low-cost Azure environment; scale down nonessential workers outside market hours; use masked data.
  • Staging: Production-like paper flow, test broker adapters and static-egress validation; no live capital.
  • Production: Isolated credentials, fixed egress IP, backups, alerting, rollback, gated trading features and operational support.

Long-lived WebSocket ingestion requires continuously running compute during market hours; do not assume serverless scale-to-zero is suitable for active feeds. Evaluate Azure NAT Gateway, firewall or a fixed-IP host for broker allowlisting; networking can dominate a small cloud budget.

17 / Verification

Testing and acceptance criteria

Engineering tests

  • Unit and contract tests for Express routes, Zod schemas, Python models, feature definitions and broker adapters.
  • Market replay with duplicates, out-of-order ticks, disconnects, corporate actions, missing bars and delayed news.
  • Paper OMS state-transition tests: rejected, pending, partially filled, filled, canceled and expired.
  • Ledger invariants: cash + reserved cash, realized P&L, fees, positions and no negative buying power outside allowed margin policy.
  • Load tests for quote fanout, screeners, tick aggregation and reconnect storms.
  • Security tests for broken object-level authorization, session fixation, token leakage, injection, CSRF and rate-limit bypass.
  • Backtest leakage tests, chronological splits, survivorship-bias controls, cost sensitivity and comparison with simple baselines.

Client-facing acceptance

A guest can search supported stocks, inspect licensed chart data and read dated AI rationale without creating an account.
A user can register with Google or verified email/password, create a virtual account and view all paper orders and P&L.
Autonomous paper trades occur only within configured market hours and risk constraints, with realistic execution assumptions.
Each forecast has a model version, time horizon, confidence/uncertainty, source freshness and subsequent evaluation.
Live trading cannot be activated by UI tampering or API calls while the server-side feature flag is disabled.
